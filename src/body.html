Layout

Aiming a few metres into the floor (as arenas do) lets steep stands sit right on top of the action. Aiming at the edge of play is the stricter football rule.

Nothing can be built on the pitch or this strip round it: stands, pull-out seats, roads, buildings and the plot boundary all stop at it.

Upper tiers

Let the tiers above the lower one follow their own outline, as at La Cartuja or El Monumental: the lower tier hugs the play while the stadium around it is an oval or a circle.

Stands, tiers & roofs

One roof over the whole ground

Design the roof on its own instead of stand by stand: one continuous roof that runs right round the ground, over gaps and open corners too.

Site boundary

Drag the red corners or edges in the 3D view (Plan view is easiest). Stands are cut back wherever the boundary runs, so one end can end up wedged and lopsided.

Convertible

Let the ground switch to a second sport. A bigger field gets room as the lower tier slides back under the one above (like the Stade de France) or has its front rows decked over; a smaller one brings pull-out seats forward to sit fans close.

Surroundings

Roads, rivers, canals, railways and buildings round the ground, at real sizes. Stands are cut back to clear them (untick the hard boundary under Site boundary to get warnings instead). In the 3D view, drag a yellow post's middle one to move it and its end one to turn and stretch it.

Seating sizes, gangways & exits
Access

Each stand can have its own number, place and arrangement of platforms: pick the stand under Stands, then Facilities, access and fire risk. Each wheelchair space comes with a companion seat beside it. The vehicle entrance is 4.5 m high, so an ambulance or fire engine can drive straight onto the pitch.

Safety rules

Starting values are based on UK sports-ground guidance (the Green Guide). Check the current edition with a safety engineer before relying on them.

    Signature architecture

    Big gestures that turn a set of stands into one building. They change how it looks, not the capacity.

    Floodlights & screens

    Put screens on any stand or corner: tucked between the back row and the roof (great on tall, short ends), hung from the roof edge, on top, on legs behind, or as LED ribbons along the tier fronts.

    Tunnel and dugouts

    The tunnel and dugouts sunk into a stand take the front rows out there, so the capacity drops a little. Perspex dugouts need about 4 m between the touchline and the stand.

    Name and sponsors

    Their names go on the boards round the pitch and on any LED ribbons along the tier fronts (add one under Floodlights & screens). Each sponsor gets a panel in its own colours. Naming rights: give the ground a sponsor's name and it goes up on the main stand.

    Kick-off flyover

    A 15-second swoop from high over the town, round and in over the roof, down to the touchline. Tap to stop it.

    Capacity (estimate)

    The detailed numbers (sightlines, distances, evacuation times, tier by tier) are under Stats.

    Capacity allows 5% for stairs and entrances on seated tiers and 10% for gangways on terraces. Sightline uses the C-value: how far each fan's eye sits above the eye line of the person in front, aimed at the nearest edge of play (the top of a raised ring or cage), or the set distance into the floor. A seat has a restricted view when something (a pillar, the tier above, the roof front, a mast, a screen, the dugouts) hides part of the pitch from it: slightly when it loses a corner or so, badly when it loses a big slice or a goalmouth. Emptying time assumes every exit flows at the set rate at once.
